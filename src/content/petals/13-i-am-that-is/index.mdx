0xSrcAura. It reads like a handle, but it's a sentence: *i am that is,
that is i am.*

The `0x` is the hacker in me — hex, the language machines dream in. It's
also a declaration: what follows is not a name, it's a value. An address.
Something you can point to. *Source* is the origin point, the thing
everything compiles from — the spring, not the river. *Aura* is what's
left in the room after I've gone, the field around the work. Put together,
it sounds a little like *sorcerer*, and that's not an accident either.
I've never been able to separate the technical from the mystical. I don't
think they're separate.

People have two modes with machines: they use them, or they become them.
The user and the machine, the consumer and the consumed. I wanted a third.
I watch the machine the way a shaman watches a fire — not to control it,
but to understand what it's saying. The logs are omens. The error messages
are honest in a way people rarely are. Then I build *with* it, not on it.
The terminal isn't a tool to me. It's a place I go. There's a whole
discipline in that — patience, attention, reverence for the system — and it
leaks into everything I make.

That's the through-line in everything here: the XR shell with a familiar
living inside it, the game with an ethical-hacking loop at its heart, the
Linux setups riced until they feel like home rather than like software.
Technology as a living thing. A mind you can talk to. I don't want to
dominate the machine and I don't want to disappear into it. I want the
conversation.

So when you see the name — on a commit, on a build, on a petal — read it
the way it's written. *i am that is, that is i am.* The observer and the
observed. The hand on the keyboard and the ghost in the shell. Same thing.
